# Design system

Calm, pastel, legible. The site exists to make the DSM-5-TR understandable to a nervous 14-year-old at midnight and a worried parent on a lunch break. Every decision below serves three rules: **nothing alarming, nothing clinical-looking, nothing you can't read.** Tokens live in `data/design-tokens.json` and are generated by `tools/build_design_tokens.py`, which checks every text/background pair against WCAG 2.2 AA.

## 1. Mood and principles

- **Soft, not sterile.** Warm off-white page (`#fbfaf7`), white cards, one pastel tint per chapter. Think a well-lit library, not a hospital corridor.
- **One accent per screen.** The chapter colour carries the page. Everything else is neutral ink and line.
- **No red, except the crisis strip.** Red is reserved so that when it appears it means one thing: help, now.
- **Illustrated, not photographed.** Soft line illustrations of people doing ordinary things in daylight. Never stock photos of heads in hands.
- **Readable first.** Body text 17px, line-height 1.6, max 66 characters per line, Atkinson Hyperlegible.
- **Quiet motion.** 200 ms ease-out transitions; none at all under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Status colours (never red)

Info (blue), success (green), caution (amber) each have a background and ink pair per theme. Use caution amber for "needs verification" badges and medication notes, never for anything about the reader's safety.

## 3. Typography

- **Body:** Atkinson Hyperlegible (free, designed for low vision), fallback Inter / system-ui. 17px base, 1.6 line-height.
- **Plain title:** Fraunces (variable serif) at 30–38px, slight negative tracking. Warm, human, not a headline font.
- **Official name:** 15px, ink secondary, in sentence case with the ICD-10-CM code in mono beside it.
- **Section labels** ("In simple words", "What helps"): 13px, semibold, letter-spacing 0.06em, chapter accent.
- Scale: xs 13 / sm 15 / base 17 / lg 20 / xl 24 / 2xl 30 / 3xl 38 (px equivalents of the rem tokens).
- Measure: 66ch for body, 58ch inside cards. Never justify text. Never all-caps for more than three words.

## 4. Spacing, radius, elevation

- 4px base unit. Card padding 24px, card gap 16px, page gutter 16px mobile / 32px desktop.
- Radius: cards 20px, chips pill, buttons 14px, inputs 12px. Softness is part of the tone.
- Shadows are faint and warm (`0 8px 24px rgba(35,39,47,0.06)`); on dark theme use the `dark_card` token. No hard borders on cards; use the chapter `border` token only for focus rings and the crisis strip.

## 5. Card anatomy

A card page is one topic, laid out top to bottom. Mobile is single column; from `md` the "You're not alone" module and glossary sit in a right rail.

1. **Header band** on the chapter tint: chapter icon (Lucide, 28px, accent) and chapter plain name as a breadcrumb; plain title (Fraunces); official name and code; "also known as" chips; language-convention note as a muted line when present.
2. **Sensitivity badge** (elevated or high) immediately under the header, using `chip` with the `life-buoy` icon; for high, the crisis background with `heart-handshake`.
3. **Crisis strip** (see section 7) pinned directly below the header on elevated and high cards, and sticky at the bottom of the viewport on high cards.
4. **One-liner** in large body size, then **In simple words** as the opening paragraph.
5. **What it can feel like**: quotes in a soft tint panel, each with a quotation mark glyph in accent, no attribution (they are composites).
6. **Common signs**: a plain list with a one-line reminder above it: *"Lists like this describe, they don't diagnose. Only a clinician can do that."*
7. **Myths vs facts**: two-column on desktop, stacked on mobile; myth rows use `x-circle`, fact rows `check-circle`, both in ink muted, never red/green.
8. **How common**: the plain sentence in body text; the figure and source as a collapsed "See the number" disclosure, so numbers never dominate. Hidden entirely on high-sensitivity eating-disorder cards per safe messaging.
9. **What helps**: plain summary, then two sub-lists (evidence-based options; self-help that supports care). A caution chip *"Medication is always prescribed and monitored by a doctor"* appears automatically if any option mentions medication.
10. **When to reach out** on a raised surface with the `phone` icon; on elevated/high cards this block repeats the crisis strip's first line.
11. **What it isn't**, **Hope note** (with `sun` icon; always the last text block before community content).
12. Optional blocks, each with its icon: In young people (`backpack`), Across cultures (`globe`), Strengths and differences (`sparkles`), Sub-sections as an accordion.
13. **You're not alone** module (section 6).
14. **Related topics** as chips in their own chapters' tints; **Glossary terms** as dotted-underline links that open a popover with the plain definition.
15. **Sources** as a collapsed list; each shows a `badge-check` (verified) or `badge-help` (needs verification) icon and its `access_method`.
16. **Review footer**: "Reviewed: not yet / by whom", reading grade, "Report a problem" link.

## 6. "You're not alone" module

- Title: **You're not alone**. Subtitle: *Moderated places to talk with people who get it. Peer support, not professional advice.*
- Up to six community cards, each: platform icon (`users` for forums, `phone`/`message-square` for lines), name, one-line description from `notes`, chips for region, audience, and `for`, and a verification badge. Helplines show their number as a `tel:` link and hours.
- Cards for people with the condition come first, then family/carer cards, then resource hubs.
- A standing footer line: *Every community is re-checked regularly. If one has become unsafe, tell us.*
- Never show member counts prominently; they are in the data for reviewers, not for the reader.

## 7. Crisis strip

- Appears on every card with `show_crisis_strip: true` (elevated and high) and in the site footer everywhere.
- Content from `data/crisis-lines.json`: the visitor's country is detected (or chosen in the header) and its `priority: 1` lines are shown: emergency number, main crisis line (call and text), and the youth line if the visitor has selected "I'm under 18". Then one global fallback (Find A Helpline).
- Layout: crisis background, 1px crisis border on the left edge (4px on high cards), `life-buoy` icon, one sentence (*"If you're in danger or can't keep yourself safe right now, reach out."*), then buttons: **Call 988**, **Text HOME to 741741**, **More countries**. Buttons are `tel:` and `sms:` links. Text is never smaller than 15px.
- On **high** cards the strip is also sticky at the bottom of the viewport on mobile.
- On partner-violence and child-abuse cards a **Quick exit** button (`log-out` icon) sits in the strip and the header; it navigates to a neutral site and replaces the history entry.
- Never animate the strip. Never auto-dismiss. Never put a close button on high cards.

## 8. Illustration guide

- Style: single-weight soft line drawings, filled with two or three pastels from the chapter palette, generous white space. Think a kind children's-book illustrator working for adults.
- Subjects: people doing ordinary things in daylight, usually with one other person or an animal. Diverse ages, bodies, skin tones, and mobility aids drawn without comment.
- Always: a source of light (window, sky, lamp), an open posture, and something in progress (making tea, walking, sorting letters).
- Never: heads in hands, storm clouds, cracked masks, tangled wires, pills, scales, mirrors with distorted reflections, fire, blades, wounds, bruises, children on paraphilic or abuse cards, or any depiction of a method.
- Every card's `visual.illustration_prompt` and `alt_text` are written to this guide. Alt text describes what is shown, not the condition.

## 9. Tailwind mapping

```js
// tailwind.config.js (excerpt)
const tokens = require('./data/design-tokens.json');
module.exports = {
  darkMode: ['selector', '[data-theme="dark"]'],
  theme: {
    extend: {
      colors: {
        page: 'var(--page)', card: 'var(--card)', raised: 'var(--raised)',
        ink: { DEFAULT: 'var(--ink)', secondary: 'var(--ink-2)', muted: 'var(--ink-3)' },
        tint: 'var(--tint)', accent: 'var(--accent)', chip: 'var(--chip)',
        crisis: { bg: 'var(--crisis-bg)', border: 'var(--crisis-border)', ink: 'var(--crisis-ink)' },
      },
      fontFamily: { sans: tokens.typography.font_family.sans.split(','), display: tokens.typography.font_family.display.split(',') },
      borderRadius: tokens.radius,
      boxShadow: { card: tokens.shadow.card, 'card-hover': tokens.shadow.card_hover },
    },
  },
};
```

Set the CSS variables once per theme on `:root` / `[data-theme="dark"]`, and per chapter on the card wrapper (`style="--tint: …; --accent: …"`) from `color.chapters[chapter_id][theme]`.

## 10. Dark mode

- Toggle in the header (`moon` / `sun`), default follows `prefers-color-scheme`, choice persisted in `localStorage`.
- Chapter tints become deep, desaturated versions of the hue (lightness 17%); accents lighten to 74%+ so they stay text-grade. Illustrations get a 90% opacity white card behind them rather than being inverted.
- The crisis strip stays recognisably red-family in both themes (see section 2).

## 11. Accessibility checklist

- All text ≥ 4.5:1, UI borders and icons ≥ 3:1 (enforced by the token generator).
- Focus ring: 3px chapter `border` colour with 2px page-colour offset, visible on every interactive element.
- Touch targets ≥ 44 × 44px. Crisis buttons ≥ 48px tall.
- Headings in order (h1 plain title, h2 sections). Landmarks: header, nav, main, aside (You're not alone), footer.
- Glossary popovers are buttons, keyboard-openable, closed with Escape, and also render inline on print.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` disables all transitions; no parallax, no autoplay.
- Text resizes to 200% without horizontal scroll (fluid type, no fixed heights).
- Language attribute per page (`lang="en"`), `dir` ready for future translations.
- Icons always paired with text or `aria-label`; the crisis strip is `role="region"` with `aria-label="Crisis help"`.
- Reading grade target 6–8 is reported per card; cards above 8 are listed in the QA report for simplification.
